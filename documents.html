Historical Overview

Tripura in Ancient Indian Traditions and Historical Records

1. Tripura in the Mahabharata

The Mahabharata, one of the major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, contains a passage in the Sabha Parva describing Sahadeva's campaign and a ruler associated with Tripura. This reference is part of the epic tradition connected with the region. However, the identity of the epic's Tripura and the historical date of the events cannot be established from this passage alone.

Reference: Mahabharata, Sabha Parva, Book 2, Chapter 28, Verse 38 (commonly used numbering). Read the verse.

2. The Prayaga Prashasti of Samudragupta

The fourth-century CE Prayaga Prashasti, inscribed on the pillar at present-day Prayagraj, records frontier states including Samatata, Davaka, Kamarupa and Nepala, as well as a place-name commonly transcribed as Kartripura. Its identification remains debated, and it should not automatically be equated with Tripura. This inscription belongs to the Gupta emperor Samudragupta, not to Emperor Ashoka.

Further reading: Siddham inscription record.

3. Rajmala: The Royal Chronicle of Tripura

Rajmala, a royal chronicle composed in Bengali verse by scholars associated with Tripura's royal court, preserves dynastic traditions, genealogies and accounts of rulers. It is an important source for understanding Tripura's historical memory, although early genealogies and legendary narratives should be compared with inscriptions and other historical evidence.

Reference: James Long, Analysis of the Raj-mala, or Chronicles of Tripura (1850). Read the digitised work.

4. Tripura and the Wider History of Northeast India

Tripura's history forms part of the wider history of Northeast India, shaped by indigenous communities, political formations and cultural exchange. Accounts attributed to the Chinese Buddhist traveller Xuanzang should be checked against a reliable edition of his travel record before being quoted as direct evidence about Tripura or the peoples of the Brahmaputra valley.

5. Historical Assessment

Epic literature, royal chronicles, inscriptions and archaeological evidence offer different perspectives on Tripura's past. These sources must be assessed according to their dates, purposes and limitations. Respect for traditional accounts and careful evaluation of historical evidence together provide a more reliable understanding of Tripura's heritage.
